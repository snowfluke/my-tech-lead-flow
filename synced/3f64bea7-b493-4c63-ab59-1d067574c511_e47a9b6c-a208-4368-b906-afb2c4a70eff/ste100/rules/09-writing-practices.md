# Writing practices

Apply Section 9 (Rules 9.1-9.4); see also [rules/general-recommendations.md](general-recommendations.md) for the advisory GR items in this same section of the standard. A straight word-for-word swap from the dictionary's approved alternatives only works when the alternative shares the same part of speech and preserves meaning. Restructure the sentence instead when: the approved alternative is a different part of speech; a literal substitution reads as nonsense or bad grammar; the alternative technically substitutes but shifts the meaning; or no dictionary word covers the needed term at all. This can mean splitting a long sentence, dropping non-essential information, or moving conditional information into a table heading — always re-check the rest of the text for any effect of a local change.

Use each approved word only in its approved meaning and part of speech, even where the word has other common English senses or a restricted approved context; do not default to general usage. Do not create a phrasal verb out of two individually approved words (a verb-plus-particle combination whose combined meaning differs from its literal parts) — the dictionary generally does not flag these, so the writer must catch them; a small number of phrasal verbs are explicitly approved with a restricted meaning. Once you pick a term or sentence pattern for a recurring action or component, reuse that exact term and pattern every time it recurs; do not vary it even where each variant would individually be valid STE.
