# General recommendations (GR-1 through GR-8)

These appear in Section 9 of ASD-STE100 Issue 9 as General Recommendations (GR). The standard itself frames them as advisory guidance to help writers avoid common errors, not as numbered mandatory rules like 8.x or 9.x — treat a GR deviation as a style note, not a `BLOCKER`/`MAJOR` finding, unless it produces a genuine ambiguity that a numbered rule also covers.

- **GR-1 - "That"**: Keep the connective "that" between a main clause and a subordinate clause instead of dropping it as in casual speech; it marks the clause boundary and helps translation into languages that cannot omit their equivalent word.
- **GR-2 - "With"**: "With" carries several approved senses (association, help/sharing, means/instrument) that can make a sentence genuinely ambiguous. Reread any "with" sentence to confirm one reading; consider restating a co-occurring condition as its own leading clause instead, and make sure the sentence's primary verb, not a secondary one, still carries the main action.
- **GR-3 - Pronouns**: Use a pronoun only when it clearly points to one already-established noun; if more than one noun could be the antecedent, repeat the noun instead. Use only pronouns that are themselves approved in the dictionary — gendered pronouns ("he"/"she") are excluded (see GR-7).
- **GR-4 - "This"**: Apply the same unambiguous-antecedent test specifically to "this" — if it could point to more than one prior item, restate the referent explicitly rather than leaving a bare "this."
- **GR-5 - False friends**: A false friend is a word that looks or sounds like a word in another language but carries a different meaning in English. Verify a word's actual English meaning rather than assuming it matches a similar-looking word in your own language.
- **GR-6 - Latin abbreviations**: Avoid Latin abbreviations (for example, the common short forms for "for example," "that is," "and so on"); spell out the English equivalent, or omit the aside if it is not strictly necessary.
- **GR-7 - Inclusive language** (new in Issue 9): Use neutral, non-discriminatory language for equal representation and respect. Gender-specific pronouns ("he"/"she") and the words "man"/"woman" are not permitted, except where the applicable context genuinely requires them (for example, certain medical text). STE does not supply its own worked examples here; consult the organization's style guide or the external references the standard cites (an EU gender-sensitive-communication toolkit and a UN disability-inclusive-communications guide) for further guidance.
- **GR-8 - Possessive form (Saxon genitive)** (new in Issue 9): The apostrophe-s possessive is permitted but should be used cautiously, consistent with STE's simplicity goal — many readers' languages lack an equivalent construction or use a different one. If you are not confident the possessive is correctly formed, prefer a rewrite (for example, an "of" construction). Defer to the project's own publication specification or style guide where one governs possessive form.
