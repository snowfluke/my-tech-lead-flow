# Procedural writing

Apply Section 5 (Rules 5.1-5.5). Use no more than 20 words per procedural sentence, including warnings, cautions, and other safety instructions. Put one instruction in each sentence, normally numbered in sequence, unless two or more actions genuinely occur at the same time or an immediate result is inseparable from the action in that same work step. Use the imperative form, not passive or descriptive phrasing, which leaves the reader unsure whether a step is mandatory. Do not add "must" before an imperative unless the step is safety-critical or states an important condition.

Put a prerequisite condition first as a descriptive clause, then a comma, then the command; check comma placement carefully, since moving it can change which word an adverb or clause modifies. Notes contain information only, never an instruction, requirement, limit, or the imperative form — if it reads as a command it belongs in a numbered step, not a note — and each note sentence has a maximum of 25 words. Move any limit, tolerance, or step result out of a note and into the relevant work step; move any safety-critical content out of a note and into a WARNING or CAUTION. A useful check: the procedure must remain fully doable if every note were deleted.
