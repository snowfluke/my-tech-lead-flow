# Source fidelity

Compare source and output line by line — not paragraph-by-paragraph, since a dropped clause inside a kept sentence is the most common way meaning is lost silently. Confirm that every requirement, prohibition, condition, exception, limit, unit, identifier, result, sequence, and safety consequence remains present and has the same force (a weakened "shall"/"must," a prohibition softened into a suggestion, or a limit rounded or dropped is a meaning change even if the sentence still reads fluently). Flag any addition, omission, ambiguity, or invented agent/fact not in the source. Per [audit/00-severity.md](00-severity.md): a change that alters technical meaning or safety force is `BLOCKER`; an addition/omission that does not change meaning but is still unfaithful to the source (added hedging, dropped redundant-but-harmless detail) is `MAJOR`.
