# Words

Apply ASD-STE100 Section 1 (Rules 1.1-1.14). Use approved dictionary entries only with the listed part of speech, approved meaning, and dictionary-listed inflected forms (verb tenses; adjective comparative/superlative). An adjective that takes "more"/"most" has no separate listed comparative/superlative form — that is expected, not an omission.

A word not in the dictionary is usable only if it fits a technical-noun or technical-verb category for the applicable subject field; the dictionary excludes these terms because they are too numerous and field-specific to list. When selecting a technical noun: prefer your company's/industry's own already-established term over inventing one; keep it short (three words or fewer) and immediately understandable, relying on illustrations or index numbers rather than modifiers; never pick a regional, slang, or jargon term; and use exactly one term per item throughout a document, never alternating synonyms. Colors are treated as technical nouns, not adjectives, and take no comparative/superlative form. A technical noun stays a noun (never verbed); a technical verb stays a verb (never used as a noun, though its approved past-participle form may function as an adjective). Where an approved dictionary verb already expresses the action, use it instead of a technical verb — technical verbs cover only actions the dictionary cannot express, and only in the context that justifies them.

Use American English spelling unless a higher-priority directive applies; quoted/literal text is exempt and keeps its original spelling. Keep the dictionary and project glossary external and authoritative — do not invent approval decisions.
