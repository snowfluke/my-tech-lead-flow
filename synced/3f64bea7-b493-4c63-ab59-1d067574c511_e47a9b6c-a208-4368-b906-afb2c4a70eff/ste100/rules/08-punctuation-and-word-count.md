# Punctuation and word count

Apply Section 8 (Rules 8.1-8.7). Standard English punctuation is allowed except the semicolon, which is banned outright because it enables overlong, often-misused sentences — split into two sentences instead. Hyphenate words that function together as one technical unit (a compound adjective before a noun, a two-word fraction or number, a letter/number-plus-noun shape designator, a compound verb, or a prefix ending in a vowel joined to a root starting with a vowel); a dash is a different, longer mark used for ranges, not a hyphen. Parentheses are approved only for: referencing an illustration or other text; enclosing an item's identifier letter/number; marking a procedure's work-step number; enclosing an abbreviation; showing singular/plural together, e.g. "component(s)"; adding a short clarifying aside; or presenting an alternative.

STE gives its own word-counting rules; do not defer word counting to an external publication convention. A colon introducing a vertical list closes the lead-in sentence the same way a period does (lead-in capped at 20 words if procedural, 25 if descriptive); each item after the colon is then its own sentence under the same caps. A whole parenthetical insertion counts as one word in its containing sentence, while the words inside the parentheses are separately counted as their own sentence under the applicable cap. Count each of the following as a single word: a number, spelled out or as a digit (document/paragraph/step numbering itself is not counted at all); a number combined with its unit of measurement; an abbreviation, acronym, or initialism, including one combined with a number; an alphanumeric identifier; quoted or otherwise marked-off text such as an on-screen label, control name, or formula; a title, heading, placard, or label, however long, because its wording cannot be changed; and a full proper noun of a person, organization, or geopolitical entity. A hyphenated word group that functions as one adjective also counts as one word.
