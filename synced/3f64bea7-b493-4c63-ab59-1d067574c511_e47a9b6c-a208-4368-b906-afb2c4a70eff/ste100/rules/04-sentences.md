# Sentences

Apply Section 4 (Rules 4.1-4.5). Procedural sentences give one direct instruction in the imperative. Descriptive sentences cover exactly one topic each, built up gradually across sentences rather than crammed together, and must state concrete values and conditions rather than vague generalities. Never omit articles, subjects, verbs, or other necessary words to save length, and never use contractions — both damage clarity. Use dictionary-approved connecting words and phrases (and, but, then, thus, as a result, at the same time) to link a sentence to the one before or after it, mainly in descriptive text but also in procedures when a step needs a linked explanation or safety clause.

Use a vertical list when a sentence would otherwise cram in many items or steps: introduce it with a colon, mark items consistently (dash, bullet, letter, or number per house style), capitalize the first letter of each item, and end an item with a period only if it is a complete sentence — never a comma or semicolon. Keep nested sub-lists consistently indented. Repeat a negative safety command (`DO NOT`) on each item it applies to rather than stating it once above the list, and never mix procedural-type and descriptive-type items in the same list.

Use an article (a/the) or demonstrative (this/these) before a noun whenever grammatically applicable; dropping them to save words creates ambiguity. Do not use an article before a noun referring to a general or unspecified category. In a short sentence with one or two nouns, put an article before each noun; in a longer series, an article stated once before the first noun in the series may cover the whole series if that reading is unambiguous. Do not put a definite article before a noun immediately followed by an alphanumeric identifier (for example, "Tag circuit breaker 36L7," not "Tag the circuit breaker 36L7").
