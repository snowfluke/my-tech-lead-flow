# Severity definitions

Apply these definitions consistently across every audit file and every finding. A finding without a defensible severity is itself a defect in the audit — do not report an unclassified finding.

- **`BLOCKER`**: The text changes technical meaning, weakens or removes a requirement/prohibition/limit, misclassifies or hides safety-critical content (a hazard control left in a `NOTE`, a WARNING under-classified as a CAUTION or vice versa, a missing consequence for a real injury/death risk), invents an agent or fact not in the source, or uses an unapproved word/meaning that changes what the reader is told to do. A document with an open `BLOCKER` is never `STE-aligned` or `STE audit passed`, regardless of how many other checks pass.
- **`MAJOR`**: The text violates a numbered STE rule (Sections 1-9) in a way that does not itself change technical meaning but would mislead, confuse, or fail an international/non-native reader — a banned tense or auxiliary construction, a contraction, a semicolon, a sentence over its word limit, an unapproved word used where an approved one exists, a technical noun used as a verb, synonym drift for the same item, a note containing an instruction, a vertical list mixing procedural and descriptive items. `MAJOR` findings block a clean `STE audit passed` result but do not necessarily block a working draft from moving to the next revision pass.
- **`MINOR`**: A stylistic or advisory deviation that does not obscure meaning and is not a numbered-rule violation — a General Recommendation (GR-1 through GR-8) departure that creates no genuine ambiguity, an inconsistent but unambiguous punctuation choice, a preference the publication specification does not mandate. Report `MINOR` findings for the record; they do not by themselves block `STE audit passed`, but an accumulation of `MINOR` findings on the same recurring pattern should be escalated to `MAJOR` (see [rules/09-writing-practices.md](../rules/09-writing-practices.md) on consistency).
- **`UNVERIFIED`**: A vocabulary, part-of-speech, approved-meaning, or technical-noun/verb-category decision that could not be checked because no lawful dictionary source or authorized checker was available (see [dictionary/README.md](../dictionary/README.md) and [dictionary/adapter.md](../dictionary/adapter.md)). Never silently resolve an `UNVERIFIED` item as approved or not-approved. `UNVERIFIED` findings block `STE audit passed` unless the release record explicitly accepts them by name with a reason and an accountable approver.

## Escalation and downgrade rules

- Do not downgrade a `BLOCKER` to `MAJOR` to make a gate pass. If a finding's severity is genuinely uncertain, keep the higher severity and record the uncertainty in the finding's evidence/correction field.
- A finding may be downgraded from `MAJOR`/`UNVERIFIED` to accepted-with-record only through the final gate's explicit exception process (see [audit/09-final-gate.md](09-final-gate.md)), never by omitting it from the report.
- A mechanical scan (`scripts/audit_ste.py`) finding is a candidate severity, not a final one — a human/agent reviewer must confirm or reclassify it against the source text and the applicable rule before it counts toward the gate. Do not accept a script exit code alone as evidence of severity.
