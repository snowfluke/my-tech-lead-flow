# Safety instructions

Apply Section 7 (Definitions, Rules 7.1-7.3). A safety instruction warns that a risk of injury, death, or damage exists. STE defines two severity levels: WARNING (risk of injury or death) and CAUTION (risk of damage to objects or equipment only); if both risk types are present, use WARNING. Other signal words or graphical symbols (danger, attention, notice, ISO/ANSI hazard symbols) are acceptable house-style choices as long as the content still satisfies Rules 7.1-7.3 — STE does not mandate WARNING/CAUTION as the only vocabulary.

Do an honest risk assessment before assigning the signal word; under-classifying a real injury/death risk as a CAUTION is a substantive error, not a style choice. Lead with a clear, direct command or, where a prior condition matters, the condition first and then the command (mirrors the condition-comma-command structure in [rules/05-procedural-writing.md](05-procedural-writing.md)); use an explicit negative command such as `DO NOT` where prohibition is required. Wherever possible, follow the command with the concrete risk or consequence of not obeying it, so the reader understands why it matters. Put the safety control where the reader needs it, not in a note (see [rules/05-procedural-writing.md](05-procedural-writing.md)), and keep each safety sentence within the 20-word procedural limit from Section 5. Preserve required signal words and exact quoted safety text, and never weaken a stated prohibition or requirement during an STE rewrite.
